Achilles' heel isn't in the Iliad.

Homer's epic never says Achilles was invincible.

In fact, he gets wounded. A spear grazes his arm and draws blood.

And his death? It isn't in the Iliad at all. The poem ends before it.

The heel story shows up about eight hundred years later, in a Roman poem by Statius.

His mother Thetis dipped him in the river Styx, holding him by the heel.

That one dry spot was the only place he could be hurt.

Paris's arrow found it.

And in sixteen ninety-three, a Flemish anatomist named the tendon after him. The Achilles tendon.
