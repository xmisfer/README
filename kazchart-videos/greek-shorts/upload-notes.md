## 1. Medusa wasn't born a monster (0:39)
**Hook:** "Medusa wasn't born a monster."

**Title options**

**Description:** In Ovid's version, Medusa was a beautiful young woman until Athena cursed her. And Pegasus was born from her neck. #shorts #greekmythology #medusa #mythology #history #historylab

**Facts used**
- Ovid, *Metamorphoses* Book 4 (completed c. 8 AD), describes Medusa as a beautiful maiden famous for her hair. Neptune (Poseidon) violated her in Minerva's (Athena's) temple, and Minerva punished Medusa by turning her hair into snakes.
- Earlier, Hesiod presents the Gorgons as monsters from birth. The "born beautiful" story is Ovid's, which is why the video says "In the Roman poet Ovid's version".
- Pegasus (and Chrysaor) sprang from Medusa's neck when Perseus beheaded her (Hesiod; Ovid).
- Athena wore the Gorgon's head (the gorgoneion) on her aegis or breastplate.

## 2. Pandora never had a box (0:44)
**Hook:** "Pandora never opened a box."

**Title options**
- Pandora Never Had a Box #shorts
- "Pandora's Box" Is a Translation Mistake #shorts
- What Was Really Inside Pandora's Jar #shorts

**Description:** In Hesiod it was a jar, a pithos. "Box" is a mistranslation from 1508. #shorts #greekmythology #pandora #mythology #history #historylab

**Facts used**
- Hesiod's *Works and Days* (c. 700 BC) uses *pithos*, a large storage jar.
- Diogenes the Cynic was said to live in a pithos (Diogenes Laërtius). The video says "was said to".
- Erasmus of Rotterdam, in his *Adagia* (1508), rendered it as *pyxis* (box), and that version spread.
- In Hesiod, Pandora is the first woman, made by Hephaestus on Zeus's orders as punishment after Prometheus stole fire.
- What escaped includes sickness and "hard toil". Only Elpis (Hope) remained under the lid. Whether hope was a blessing or a curse is a long-running scholarly debate.

## 3. Achilles' heel isn't in the Iliad (0:46)
**Hook:** "Achilles' heel isn't in the Iliad."

**Title options**
- Achilles' Heel Isn't in the Iliad #shorts
- The Achilles Heel Myth Is 800 Years Too Late #shorts
- Homer Never Said Achilles Was Invincible #shorts

**Description:** Homer never made Achilles invincible. The heel story first appears in a Roman poem about 800 years later. #shorts #greekmythology #achilles #iliad #history #historylab

**Facts used**
- The *Iliad* (attributed to Homer, c. 8th century BC) never mentions invulnerability or a heel. In Book 21, Asteropaeus's spear grazes Achilles' forearm and draws blood.
- The *Iliad* ends with Hector's funeral. Achilles' death by Paris's arrow came in the lost *Aethiopis* and later sources.
- The dipping in the Styx, held by the heel, first appears in Statius's *Achilleid* (c. 95 AD).
- The Flemish anatomist Philip Verheyen named the "chorda Achillis" (Achilles tendon) in 1693.
