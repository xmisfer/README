## 1. The flood before Noah (0:57)
**Hook:** "Centuries before the story of Noah was written down, ancient Iraq already had a flood hero."

**Title options**
- The Flood Story Older Than Noah #shorts
- Ancient Iraq's Flood Hero Built a Giant CUBE #shorts
- The Gods Were Cowering Like Dogs #shorts

**Description:** Utnapishtim, the flood hero of the Epic of Gilgamesh: a secret warning, a cube-shaped boat, and a raven that never came back. #shorts #mesopotamia #sumer #gilgamesh #ancienthistory #historylab

**Facts used**
- The flood story appears in *Atrahasis* (c. 18th century BC), in a Sumerian version with Ziusudra, and in Tablet XI of the Standard Babylonian *Epic of Gilgamesh*, where the hero is Utnapishtim. These versions are dated centuries before the biblical flood narrative was written down (by mainstream scholarly dating).
- The god Ea secretly warns Utnapishtim through a reed wall: "Tear down your house, build a boat."
- The boat's length and width are equal: a cube about 120 cubits (roughly 60 m) on each side.
- The storm lasts six days and seven nights. "The gods cowered like dogs."
- The boat lands on Mount Nimush. He releases a dove and a swallow, which return, and then a raven, which does not.
- George Smith of the British Museum translated the flood tablet in 1872. A colleague reported that he was so excited he began to undress. The video says "reportedly".

## 2. Gilgamesh, the king who feared death (0:50)
**Hook:** "The world's oldest epic is about a king who was terrified of dying."

**Title options**
- The World's Oldest Story Is About Fearing Death #shorts
- A Snake Stole the Secret of Eternal Youth #shorts
- Gilgamesh Almost Lived Forever #shorts

**Description:** Gilgamesh found the plant of youth at the bottom of the sea, and a snake stole it. 4,000 years later, we still know his name. #shorts #gilgamesh #mesopotamia #sumer #mythology #historylab

**Facts used**
- The *Epic of Gilgamesh* is generally considered the oldest surviving great work of literature. Sumerian Gilgamesh poems date from about 2100 BC.
- Gilgamesh is king of Uruk and "two-thirds god, one-third human". A historical king of that name likely ruled around 2700 BC.
- After his friend Enkidu dies, Gilgamesh searches for immortality.
- Utnapishtim tells him of a thorny plant at the bottom of the sea, called "Old Man Becomes Young". He ties stones to his feet, sinks down and pulls it up.
- While he bathes in a pool, a snake smells the plant and carries it off, shedding its skin as it goes.
- He weeps, returns to Uruk and shows the boatman Urshanabi the city walls.

## 3. Inanna's descent to the underworld (0:51)
**Hook:** "A goddess went to the underworld, and sent her husband to take her place."

**Title options**
- She Sent Her Husband to Hell Instead #shorts
- The 4,000-Year-Old Myth of Inanna's Descent #shorts
- A Goddess Was Hung on a Hook #shorts

**Description:** Inanna, queen of heaven, passed seven gates into the underworld, and only one person could take her place. #shorts #inanna #sumer #mesopotamia #mythology #historylab

**Facts used** (Sumerian poem *Inanna's Descent to the Netherworld*)
- Inanna goes down to the underworld of her sister Ereshkigal. At each of the seven gates she must give up an item: her crown, beads and necklace, breastplate, ring, measuring rod and line, and robe.
- She arrives naked before the Anunna judges. Ereshkigal fixes "the eye of death" on her, and her corpse is hung on a hook for three days and nights.
- Enki creates two beings, the kurgarra and galatur, from dirt under his fingernails to revive her with food and water of life.
- A substitute must take her place. She finds her husband Dumuzi on his throne in fine clothes, not mourning her, and chooses him.
- In the end Dumuzi and his sister Geshtinanna each spend half the year in the underworld.
