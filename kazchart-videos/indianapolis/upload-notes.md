## Description
In July 1945, the USS Indianapolis delivered parts of the atomic bomb. Four days later, a Japanese submarine sank her in twelve minutes. Nearly 900 men went into the Philippine Sea, and no one knew they were missing.

## Facts used (check before publishing)
- 1,195 crew aboard. About 300 died in the sinking and about 890 went into the water. 316 survived.
- Delivered atomic bomb (Little Boy) components to Tinian on 26 July 1945. Hiroshima was bombed on 6 August.
- Sailed unescorted. Torpedoed by the Japanese submarine I-58 at about 00:14 on 30 July. Two torpedoes hit and she sank in about 12 minutes.
- No one reported her overdue. A PV-1 Ventura on routine patrol spotted the survivors by chance on 2 August, the fourth day.
- Most deaths came from exposure, dehydration and saltwater poisoning. Oceanic whitetip attacks are well documented, but estimates of how many died from them vary widely, so the video gives no number.
- Worst loss of life at sea from a single ship in US Navy history.
- Capt. Charles B. McVay III was court-martialed in December 1945 for hazarding his ship. Congress exonerated him in October 2000. He died in 1968.
- The wreck was found in August 2017 at about 5,500 m (18,000 ft).
