## 1. Vikings never wore horned helmets (0:41)
**Hook:** "Vikings never wore horned helmets."

**Title options**
- Vikings NEVER Wore Horned Helmets #shorts

- The Only Real Viking Helmet Ever Found #shorts

**Description:** The only near-complete Viking helmet ever found has no horns. Those came from an 1876 opera costume designer. #shorts #vikings #norsemythology #history #historylab

**Facts used**
- The Gjermundbu helmet, found in Norway in 1943 and dated to the 10th century, is the only near-complete Viking Age helmet known. It is a rounded iron cap with a "spectacle" eye guard and no horns.
- Carl Emil Doepler designed horned helmets for the first Bayreuth production of Wagner's *Der Ring des Nibelungen* in 1876, which fixed the image in popular culture.
- The Veksø helmets (Denmark, c. 900 BC) are real Bronze Age horned helmets, about 1,700 years before the Viking Age (c. 793–1066 AD).

## 2. Loki isn't Thor's brother (0:47)
**Hook:** "Loki isn't Thor's brother."

**Title options**
- Loki Is NOT Thor's Brother #shorts
- Loki Gave Birth to Odin's Horse #shorts
- The Weirdest Story in Norse Mythology #shorts

**Description:** Forget Marvel. In the old Norse poems Loki is Odin's blood-brother, and the mother of Odin's eight-legged horse. #shorts #norsemythology #loki #odin #mythology #historylab

**Facts used**
- Loki is the son of the giant Fárbauti and Laufey (Snorri's *Prose Edda*). In *Lokasenna* (Poetic Edda, stanza 9) he reminds Odin that they once mixed their blood. The Thor-and-Loki brothers relationship comes from Marvel.
- In *Gylfaginning*, a giant builder's stallion, Svaðilfari, works too fast on the gods' wall. Loki turns into a mare to lure it away and later gives birth to Sleipnir, the eight-legged horse Odin rides, called the best of horses.
- Loki's children with Angrboða are Fenrir the wolf, Jörmungandr the world serpent and Hel, ruler of the dead.
- The Poetic Edda survives in the Codex Regius, written c. 1270 in Iceland. The poems themselves are older.

**Title options**

**Description:** Fimbulwinter, Fenrir, the world serpent, and a world on fire, then a new beginning. #shorts #ragnarok #norsemythology #thor #odin #historylab

**Facts used** (Snorri's *Gylfaginning* and the *Völuspá* / *Vafþrúðnismál* poems)
- Ragnarök means roughly "the fate (or doom) of the gods".
- Fimbulwinter comes first: three winters in a row with no summer between.
- Fenrir breaks free and swallows Odin. Thor kills Jörmungandr, walks nine steps and falls dead from its venom. Loki and Heimdall kill each other. The fire giant Surtr burns the world.
- The earth rises again from the sea, green. Two humans, Líf and Lífþrasir, survive hidden in the wood of Hoddmímir and repopulate the world.
